On 4 June 1996, Europe’s new Ariane 5 rocket lifted off on its first flight. About 37 seconds later it was in pieces,
along with roughly $370 million of rocket and satellites.

The cause wasn’t an engine or a weld. It was one line of code that converted a number into a box too small to hold it.

You can do it yourself, right now:

<OverflowDemo />

Move the slider past 32,767 and you’ve recreated the failure. That’s the whole idea of this series.

## What Post-Mortem Lab is

Every episode takes one real engineering failure and rebuilds it as a small, faithful simulation you can run in your
browser. You get to:

1. **Hear the story.** What happened, who was affected, and why smart people missed it.
2. **Trigger the bug.** Not a diagram of it. The actual mechanism, running.
3. **Fix it.** And see why the fix works, with tests that prove it.
4. **Keep the lesson.** One idea you’ll recognise next time it shows up in your own code.

All the code is open source. Every episode is a tagged release, so you can check out exactly what you saw in the video.

## How it’s built

Everything sits on a shared toolkit that grows with every episode: checked number conversions, fault injection,
deterministic timing, unit-aware maths. By the end of the season it will be a small library for *safely causing
failures*, useful well beyond these episodes.

I build it with AI as my pair programmer, and I’ll show that honestly, including the parts where it
gets things wrong.
